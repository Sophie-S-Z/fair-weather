# ADR 0013: Part 2 rules for the bridge, three valleys, and the homecoming

**Status:** Accepted. The content tasks (M3.8 to M3.10) add the calibration they need to items 16 to 18.

## Context

Milestone M3 builds the bridge, Murmuration Fen, Hearsay Hollow, Halcyon, the homecoming, the travel journal, reputation, and postcards (spec 7, 9.5, 9.6). The spec names these features but leaves gaps: how a player's hero acts in a meeting that resolves in one tick, where knacks and rule packs touch the meeting rules, what a reputation rumor says, how Kith runs while the player is away, when Wisp chooses, and how a valley ends. Spec 5.5 also gives the Bellchime a reach of 6 world units, which is less than the 24-unit meeting radius, so it would reach no one. ADR 0007 holds the earlier Part 2 defaults; this ADR adds to them and wins where the two differ.

## Decision

1. **The hero is one creature, id `hero`.** Its `speciesId` is its hero kind (`lantern`, `kite`, `mosscoat`, `glassling`, `seedcarrier`, `bellchime`), its `name` is the player's, and its dials are 0.5. Every meeting, fog, payoff, warmth, shell, eating, and foraging rule applies to it (spec 7.2). It never picks a goal of its own, never leaves by hunger, and takes no part in gossip as a speaker or in imitation, so only the player moves and shapes it. Locals gossip about it as about anyone. Water stops it without a new goal. An `arrangeMeeting` that names the hero moves only the other creature.
2. **The hero's manner.** The player sets a standing manner, a reply (`toward`, `away`, `against`) and a gift move (`give`, `keep`), with the `manner` command (default `toward` and `give`). A meeting resolves in one tick, so the manner is how the player gives, keeps, and answers. The hero bids only at a meeting with the creature it waved at (`walk` with `to`); at every other meeting the local bids and the hero answers with its manner. The meeting still takes its seven draws in the same order (ADR 0003); the hero's reply and gifts ignore their draws. A hero with fewer than `gift.cost` berries keeps.
3. **Knacks (spec 5.5), exact rules.** Lantern-bearer: the fog that can misread the hero's reply or gifts is halved; in Hearsay Hollow the drama sky moves twice as fast toward a meeting that involves the hero. Kite-rider: speed × 1.5; "sees one ridge ahead" has no Stage 1 effect (Foglight Heights, M5); in Murmuration Fen the reputation rumor does not apply. Mosscoat: toward the hero, a local's slights fade with Memory `min(1, 2 × Memory)` and its warmth drifts at `knack.mossDrift` (0.5) × `warmth.dailyDrift`. Glassling: fog never misreads the hero's reply or gifts, and each meeting warmth change on a local's side toward the hero is × 1.5 (a keepsake or a gift bush gives its plain amount); in Hearsay Hollow its `away` replies score 3 drama, as an `against`. Seedcarrier: `plant` at the hero's spot costs 1 berry and makes a gift bush with 0 berries that holds 3 berries from the dusk 2 days later and never regrows; each creature that takes a berry from it gains 0.1 warmth toward the hero, once per gift. Bellchime: a wave also reaches every other wandering creature within `knack.bellRadius` (72, three meeting radii, since spec 5.5's 6 world units reach no one), each of which answers separately with one reply draw (only in worlds with a Bellchime hero); in Hearsay Hollow a print about the hero raises its fame twice.
4. **Rule packs (spec 9.5).** `src/sim/packs/` holds one pack per valley with `setup(world, t, rng)`, `step(world, events, t, rng)`, `onVerb(world, verb, t, rng)`, and `metrics(world, t)`. The spec's interface takes no tuning and no events; a pack needs the tuning for its numbers and Hearsay Hollow reads the tick's meetings, so both are passed. A pack's state lives in `World.pack`, so hashing, checkpoints, and rewind keep it. `step` runs after the dance spread and before light sky warmth. A pack changes the meeting rules only through three questions the meeting code asks: may these two meet, what a gift costs, and what a received gift is worth in warmth. A scenario without `pack` takes no pack draw, so the M0 golden hash does not move.
5. **Murmuration Fen.** 39 starlings and Tove, the guide, are creatures of species `starling`. At setup each starling draws a threshold from `fen.thresholdMin` (0.3) to 0.8 in creature order (ADR 0007 said 0.2; item 16 says why); Tove's is 0.5 and the scenario's `restless` starlings fly from the start and never land (ADR 0007 thresholds). Every `fen.everyTicks` (10) ticks, each grounded starling flies when the share of its 7 nearest other creatures (starlings and the hero) in flight reaches its threshold, lowered by `fen.duskLift` (0.15) from `fen.duskAt` (0.75) of the day; the hero counts as flying while it has joined. Every starling but the restless lands at dawn. A flying creature meets no one. The reflection (ADR 0007) is each starling's perceived share; the snapshot carries it. `join` sets the hero flying or grounded.
6. **Hearsay Hollow.** The drama sky is the lens (`fields.light`), so its light boost is the spec 9.4 rule. A meeting scores the ADR 0007 drama: an `against` reply 3, a keep after the other side's gift 2, an `away` reply 1; its subject is the creature that acted (the replier, or the keeper). `pose` scores 3 at the hero's spot with the hero as subject, without a meeting. Scores halve at each dusk. The sky moves at `hollow.skySpeed` (120 units a second) toward the highest score (ties to the lower subject id). At dusk the gazette prints the highest-scored drama within the lens that day: its subject's fame rises by `hollow.fameStep` (0.2); every fame then falls by `hollow.fameFade` (0.1 × fame). A creature with fame above `hollow.fearAt` (0.5) meets no local: the town keeps its distance.
7. **Halcyon.** A gift costs 0 berries and the received gift's warmth is × `halcyon.giftWarmthMultiplier` (0.2). There is no fog and no winter. `keepsake` gives one of the hero's keepsakes to a creature within 2 meeting radii: the hero loses it and the receiver's warmth toward the hero rises by `halcyon.keepsakeWarmth` (0.6). Wisp chooses once (spec 7.4), at the first dusk after it receives a keepsake, or else at the dusk that ends day `halcyon.chooseByDay` (3): it goes home when its warmth toward the hero is at least `wisp.minWarmth` (0.6) and the scenario's `kithWarmth` (Kith's average after the postcards so far, item 10) is at least `wisp.minKithWarmth` (0.2); otherwise it stays and gives its seed. The choice is a sim event (`goHome` or `stay`), so the story tells what the sim decided.
8. **Reputation (spec 9.6).** The record is the hero's last 20 give-or-keep moves and the bids it answered. Its rumor value is `clamp(2 × giftShare − 1 − againstShare, −1, 1)`, with `giftShare` 0.5 when the hero has made no move: all gifts read 1, half read 0, all keeps read −1, and turning bids against pulls it down. The strength is the share of the valley's locals (not the hero, not away) whose opinion of the hero is at least `opinion.holdAt` in size when the hero leaves. In the next valley, and at the homecoming, each local with Ear at or below the strength starts with the value as its opinion of the hero, so its first meeting starts from it (ADR 0002). The journal says the rumor in words; the player cannot change it.
9. **A valley is a chapter config with `kind: "valley"`.** It names its base scenario, its rule pack, its guide, its palette, two or three postcard types (none for the homecoming), and its keepsake. The chapter runner plays it, so beats, the twist window, the honesty rule, skips, and flags work as in Part 1. Each valley starts a new world; only the journey (the hero, its record, the rumor, keepsakes, journal pages, and postcards) carries between valleys. The postcard card opens from a beat after the twist, and a valley with postcards ends when one is sent. A skipped valley adds nothing to the journey but its Kith days.
10. **Kith runs while the player is away (ADR 0007, spec 7.2).** A second worker holds Kith from the end of Part 1. When a valley ends, its postcard nudges one dial of every creature in Kith by `postcard.nudge` (0.1) and Kith runs `kith.days` (20) days split over the Stage 1 valleys in play order (7, 7, 6), as one silent run with one snapshot at its end. Wisp reads Kith's average warmth after the first two valleys; the homecoming starts from Kith after all three. Continue rebuilds Kith by running the same steps again, which the deterministic sim makes exact.
11. **The bridge** is an interlude with activity `hero`: the rain falls, the player picks one of the six heroes and names it (on the device only, spec 15.4), and Pim gives the postcard map. Skipping it gives the first hero with its own name.
12. **The homecoming** is a valley in Kith (`pack` none) that starts from item 10's Kith, with the hero, and with Wisp beside the hero or Wisp's seed growing as a bush by the first bench. Its one variant reads `kithHeld` (Kith's average warmth at least `wisp.minKithWarmth`): the cast waits at the gate. Its twist is `homeWelcome`: at least 3 of the named cast hold warmth toward the hero above `friend.threshold`. The last beat seats the hero and Pim on the first bench, and Pim says how the hero's line changed, read from the hero's shell.
13. **The sixth verse** (spec 10.6 item 6) has its own file and 6 to 8 templates over the Part 2 features: the hero kind, the gift and against shares, Wisp's choice, `kithHeld`, `toveFlew`, and `heroFamous`. Its first line, "The sky came down once, and walked with us.", is the spec's own. At the end it is added to the year's page in the *Book of Skies*.
14. **The journal** draws each finished valley as a spread: a placeholder drawing of the key moment (an artist replaces it, ADR 0010), the keepsake or its empty pressed shape, the locals' notes, a stamp, one caption of the player's own (at most 80 characters, on the device only), "What they say about you", and the valley's margin notes. The stamp reads the locals' mean warmth toward the hero at the exit: welcomed at `friend.threshold` or more, forgiven at 0 or more, avoided below 0 or with no tie.
15. **Hero balance stands in for "every hero playtested in every valley"** until the human playtest (T11): for each valley and each hero, the valley's T3 player brings the twist in at least 8 of 10 seeds, and a player who never uses the local verb brings it in at most 2.
16. **Murmuration Fen calibration (M3.8).** The T3 player walks to Tove (a wave every 2 seconds) and joins from `fen.duskAt` of each day, landing at dawn; the control walks the same way and never joins. Each step below, with its numbers from the real headless runner unless it says otherwise:
    - **1. First layout, as planned** (60-s day, twist window 3 days): 39 starlings and Tove in five clusters of 8, Tove at the edge of one, five restless starlings in a far cluster, Wanderlust 0.5 and no ties. The starlings scatter within the first minute, the restless ones pass every cluster, and a daytime cascade lifts the whole flock and Tove: T3 passed, but the control fired in all 200 seeds.
    - **2. Clusters that hold** (same day and window): each starling has Wanderlust 0 and starts with a ring of friend ties (warmth 0.6 both ways) to two others in its own cluster, so it walks only toward its flockmates and no cluster drifts into another. Tove's bed held 23 starlings and Tove; the small beds 4, 4, 3 and the five restless. Thresholds stay fixed for a whole valley, so a bed of 8 fails in the seeds where none of its birds has a threshold below 0.293, the most one flier lifts at dusk (an intermediate try, run on the sim alone: 12 of 40 seeds); a bed of 23 rarely has none. Result: 3 close calls in 200 (197 fired with no retry, 3 used all three), control 0 of 200. Two faults against spec 7.3 remained: Tove flew at day 1's dusk, so the valley ended after about 45 s, not 6 to 8 minutes; and no flier was ever among Tove's 7 nearest before the hero joined, so the water never misled her.
    - **3. Kept: a long day, a flier beside Tove, and a higher threshold floor.** The day is 540 s and the twist window 1 day, so day 1's dusk starts at 405 s. One restless starling (`starling-1`) sits in the middle of Tove's bed; the other four restless form their own bed; the small beds hold 5, 4 and 3; Tove sits at her bed's north edge (433, 242), on the side away from the gate. `fen.thresholdMin` rises from ADR 0007's 0.2 to 0.3 and `fen.thresholdSpan` falls from 0.6 to 0.5, so thresholds still end at 0.8. Why the tuning had to move: from 0.2, one flier lifts every starling below 0.293 at dusk, so a restless bird anywhere near Tove's bed starts the flock in the control just as the hero does. From 0.3, one flier (1 of 7) lifts no one, by day or at dusk; two lift those below 0.436 at dusk; Tove, at 0.35 by dusk, needs three. So the restless starling alone only shows in the water, as a crowd that is not there, and the joined hero beside it makes the second flier the flock needs: complex contagion. On the sim alone this gave 0 close calls, control 0, Tove misled before the hero joined in 200 of 200, and a median of 411 s. Result in the runner: 0 close calls in 200 (all fired with no retry), the postcard at a median of 410.1 s, Tove misled before the hero joined in 200 of 200, and the control fired in 1 of 200. The `fen` tuning is read only in the Fen, and the Part 1 T3 counts and the M0 golden hash are unchanged.
    - **Retries cannot rescue the Fen.** Thresholds are fixed for a whole valley and the retry meeting (Tove and `starling-1`) does not change who flies, so a seed that misses the first window uses all three retries and plays its close call after four days. This is an honest result, not a fault of the check.

## Consequences

- The hero is a sim creature, so its body keeps its history (spec 5.2) and reputation is a sim result.
- Each rule pack and knack is on only with its scenario field, so the M0 golden hash `c29d2fa4dbda4dcf` does not change.
- A later valley (M5) adds one pack file, one config, and one scenario.
